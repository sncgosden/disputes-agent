# Product strategy

## First customer segment

Small online retailers selling physical goods, with the owner or a small operations team handling chargebacks alongside everyday work.

The initial customer problem is preparing an evidence-backed case from information scattered across payment records, orders, delivery tracking, customer messages and refunds.

## Why this segment comes first

- **A concrete workflow:** the product can follow a disputed payment through the order, fulfilment, customer communication and any refund.
- **Meaningful investigation choices:** a non-delivery complaint, an address conflict and a duplicate payment need different evidence and next actions. This creates a clear reason for agency.
- **Useful specialist boundaries:** evidence investigation, guidance interpretation, response preparation and independent review have distinct responsibilities.
- **Measurable value:** merchant preparation time, review effort, evidence coverage and appropriate escalation can be compared with the current process.
- **A focused integration path:** payment, order and fulfilment records establish a coherent starting point for connecting merchant systems.

These are selection hypotheses, not findings from merchant research. Customer research should establish how often the problem occurs, which records merchants can access and whether the product removes enough work to justify adoption.

## Initial situations

Prioritise goods not received, conflicting delivery records, duplicate or incorrect charges and missing refunds. Include cases where the evidence does not support defending the charge and cases where the right result is a question or escalation.

The broader product direction includes subscriptions, digital products and services. Each introduces different evidence requirements, so expansion should follow demonstrated customer value and case quality in the first segment.

## Alternatives considered

| Segment | Product opportunity | Reason to sequence it later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscription merchants | Investigate cancellations, recurring charges and refunds. | A strong follow-on segment, but starting here would concentrate the product around the cancellation workflow already explored in the triage project. |
| Digital-product merchants | Reconcile purchase, access and customer communication records. | Access evidence and delivery expectations need separate discovery and quality checks. |
| Service merchants | Investigate appointments, fulfilment, cancellations and quality complaints. | Service delivery varies substantially between businesses, making the initial evidence model less focused. |

## Decision principles

The product should reduce the work required to reach a sound case decision. It should not optimise for defending every charge or producing the longest response.

Measure the specialist team against a single agent and a fixed workflow using the same inputs and permissions. Keep additional agents where their contribution improves case quality, completion time or merchant effort enough to justify coordination and operating cost.

Revisit the segment choice if research shows that merchants rarely handle disputes, cannot access the required evidence, already have an adequate solution or face review effort that outweighs the time saved.
