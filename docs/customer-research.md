# Merchant customer research

## Research objective

Understand how small online retailers handle chargebacks today, where preparation becomes difficult and whether those difficulties justify a new product.

## Three core questions

### 1. What happened the last time you handled a chargeback, from receiving the notification to deciding what to do?

Topics to explore: the complaint, people involved, systems used, steps taken, deadline, time spent and outcome. If available and appropriate, a redacted case or existing checklist can make the account more concrete.

**What this reveals:** the actual workflow, who owns the work, how often the problem occurs and whether preparation is a meaningful burden.

### 2. In that case, which evidence was hardest to find or trust, and what did you do when information was missing or records disagreed?

Topics to explore: payment and order records, tracking, customer messages, refunds, manual reconciliation, unavailable records and requests for help.

**What this reveals:** evidence gaps, integration needs and decisions where adaptive investigation could help. Some problems may need better data access or a simple checklist rather than an agent.

### 3. What did handling that case cost you in time, fees or other work, and what have you already tried to make the process easier?

Topics to explore: preparation time, review time, existing services, paid tools, workarounds, unresolved frustration and control over sending responses or issuing refunds. Separate preparation burden from losses that a preparation tool could not necessarily prevent.

**What this reveals:** the value of solving the problem, alternatives the product must improve on and the controls a merchant needs before trusting automation.

## Evidence standard

Research notes distinguish observed artefacts, the merchant's account and the team's interpretation. A stated interest in AI does not establish demand. Concrete repeated problems, existing effort or spending, accessible records and willingness to try the product on an actual workflow provide stronger grounds for a product decision.

The initial segment and pain points remain hypotheses until research is completed. No merchant interviews or validated demand are claimed here.
