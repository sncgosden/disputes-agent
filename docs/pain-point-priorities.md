# Merchant pain-point priorities

## Prioritisation decision

Prioritise evidence access first, sound case judgement second and response preparation third. This order follows their dependencies: an agent cannot make a grounded recommendation without relevant records, and a polished response is useful only when its recommendation is sound.

The priorities are product hypotheses based on the current workflow map. Frequency, severity and commercial value remain unmeasured. No merchant interviews or validated demand are claimed.

| Priority | Pain-point hypothesis | Why it comes first | Proposed product response | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Finding and reconciling evidence across payment, order, delivery and customer-message systems takes significant effort. | Evidence access is a prerequisite for useful investigation. Missing identifiers or inaccessible records could prevent the product delivering value. | Locate authorised records, link them to the case, preserve sources and avoid requesting evidence already available. | Recent-case walkthroughs, observed search time, systems used, accessible identifiers and examples of missing records. |
| 2 | Deciding what conflicting or incomplete evidence supports is difficult. | This is the strongest reason for adaptive investigation: the next useful action depends on what the team discovers. Incorrect judgement can produce an unsupported response even when all records are present. | Investigate relevant claims, surface contradictions, revise the plan and ask focused questions or escalate when necessary. | Cases where evidence changed the decision, unresolved conflicts, current escalation practices and independent assessments of recommendations. |
| 3 | Turning findings into a concise, checked response under a deadline creates repeated work. | Preparation is a visible customer outcome, but it depends on the first two problems being handled. Automation that adds excessive review effort may save little time overall. | Produce a cited evidence pack and draft response, run independent review and obtain approval for external action. | Drafting and review time, existing templates or services, common corrections, provider format requirements and approval needs. |

## Assumptions and existing evidence

| Basis | What it supports | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current merchant workflow map | A coherent set of activities and dependencies to investigate. | That merchants follow this exact process or experience these problems frequently. |
| Earlier triage project's saved cancellation case | The generated answer lacked a citation and used unsupported defensive framing, demonstrating a concrete quality failure to address. | Merchant demand, the prevalence of that failure or the value of a multi-agent solution. |
| Earlier triage evaluation | A foundation for examining classification, evidence quality and review behaviour using the saved results. | Reduced merchant effort or reliable autonomous case preparation. |
| Planned merchant research | Questions capable of testing workflow, evidence difficulty, effort and alternatives. | Customer findings; interviews have not yet been conducted. |

## How these priorities could change

- If records cannot be accessed or matched reliably, prioritise the integration and data problem before expanding agent capabilities.
- If evidence reconciliation follows a predictable checklist, use a simpler workflow and reserve agency for genuinely variable investigations.
- If merchants already gather evidence efficiently but spend most time writing and checking responses, raise response preparation in priority.
- If disputes are infrequent or the current provider already solves preparation adequately, revisit the customer segment and commercial opportunity.

## Decision measures

Assess value through preparation time, review time, evidence coverage, sound recommendations and appropriate escalation. Measure costs and failures alongside successful cases. Do not infer value from agent count, draft length or acceptance alone.

Initial prioritisation uses dependency, potential customer impact and feasibility. Numerical priority scores would imply precision that the available evidence does not support. After research, update the ordering using observed frequency, effort, severity and adoption constraints.
